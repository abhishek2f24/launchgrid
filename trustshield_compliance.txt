TrustShield India Launch, Entity and Compliance Roadmap
Step-by-step business setup, registrations, compliance and launch sequence
1. Recommended Legal Positioning
Start as a private limited company providing transaction-risk assessment and verification assistance. Do not position the company as a credit bureau, detective agency, government identity authority, bank, escrow provider or payment aggregator. The first product should be a consent-based risk report and checklist engine.

3. Step-by-Step Registration Plan
Choose a clean brand name. Check MCA name availability, domain availability and trademark conflicts. Prefer a name not too generic, such as VerifyBeforePay, TrustShield, DealSafe, PaySafeCheck or BharatTrustCheck.
Hire a CA/CS firm for incorporation. This is worth doing because the product handles sensitive verification and investors/partners will care about clean compliance.
Apply for Digital Signature Certificates for proposed directors.
Reserve company name and file incorporation through MCA SPICe+ for a private limited company. SPICe+ is the MCA integrated incorporation route and covers linked registrations such as PAN/TAN and other registrations depending on the form options.
Open current bank account in the company name after certificate of incorporation.
Apply for GST if required by turnover/inter-state/service model or if payment partners/B2B clients require it early. For app/service business, taking GST early is often practical even before threshold if you plan B2B invoicing.
Apply for Startup India/DPIIT recognition after incorporation if eligible. This helps with startup credibility, government schemes and some benefits.
File trademark application for the brand and logo in relevant classes, likely software/SaaS/mobile app and verification/risk assessment services. Use a trademark lawyer for exact classes.
Register domain, business email and cloud accounts under the company, not personal accounts.
Create standard legal policies before launch: Privacy Policy, Terms of Use, Consent Notice, Refund Policy, Grievance Policy, Data Retention Policy, Law Enforcement Request Policy and Vulnerability Disclosure Policy.

5. Compliance Architecture
5.1 Personal Data and DPDP
Collect only what is necessary for a specific verification purpose.
Show a clear notice before upload: what data is collected, why, retention period, who processes it and how deletion works.
Take explicit consent before processing documents, screenshots or personal identifiers.
Allow consent withdrawal and deletion requests where legally possible.
Do not reveal one user’s documents or personal identifiers to another user.
Keep raw evidence only for a short retention period unless the user opts into longer storage.
5.2 Aadhaar, PAN and Identity Verification
Do not store Aadhaar numbers unless absolutely necessary and legally reviewed.
Do not expose Aadhaar/PAN images to another user.
For Aadhaar, prefer offline Aadhaar verification methods or licensed partner APIs. Direct online Aadhaar authentication requires participation in UIDAI’s AUA/KUA ecosystem or approved sub-entity route.
Show only derived signals such as "identity document consistency checked" or "name mismatch detected", not full identity details.
5.3 Payments and Escrow
MVP should only collect your platform fee for reports. This can be handled through a payment gateway.
Do not hold buyer funds for seller settlement in MVP. That can create payment aggregator/escrow regulatory obligations.
If you later offer escrow, use a licensed escrow/payment partner and get legal review before launch.
5.4 Intermediary and User Content Risk
Users will upload chats, documents and fraud reports. Treat this as user-generated content and moderate abuse.
Avoid a public blacklist of phone numbers or individuals. It creates defamation, privacy and false-report risk.
Keep suspicious reports internal until verified and show only risk signals, not accusations.

8. What Not To Do Initially
Do not promise "100% safe" or "verified genuine".
Do not publish private data, Aadhaar, PAN, addresses or phone numbers.
Do not create an open searchable blacklist.
Do not take custody of buyer-seller transaction funds until escrow/payment regulation is reviewed.
Do not sell user verification data to third parties.
Do not rely only on AI; use explainable rules and human review for high-risk cases.
9. Suggested Report Disclaimer
Sample language: "This report is a transaction-risk assessment based on the information provided by the user and available checks at the time of review. It is not a guarantee of identity, legality, solvency, ownership, product quality or future conduct. Users should independently verify critical information and avoid payment where material inconsistencies remain."
10. Immediate Founder Tasks
1. Pick final brand name and buy .in and .com if affordable.
2. Call CA/CS to start private limited incorporation.
3. Create one-page landing page: "Before you send money, check if the deal is risky."
4. Collect 50 real examples from WhatsApp/OLX/Facebook/Instagram scams and label the red flags.
5. Build manual MVP first: Google Form or web form + human/AI report in 10 minutes. Charge Rs. 49-99 to test willingness to pay.
6. Only after people pay for manual reports, build full app automation.
